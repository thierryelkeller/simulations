Dipoles & Compasses

Observe how magnets interact. Add a second magnet or rotate them to see the magnetic fields combine.

Hint: Drag the magnets to move them, and drag the circular handles at their ends to rotate them.

Inquiry Questions

  • How does the field change when two magnets face North-to-North versus North-to-South?
  • Where is the magnetic field strongest around a single bar magnet, and how do you know?
  • What happens to the compass arrow when you place it exactly halfway between two repelling magnets?
  • Do magnetic field lines ever cross each other? Why or why not?
  • How does rotating one magnet affect a compass that is close to the other magnet?

Earth & Auroras

Earth's magnetic field forms the Van Allen radiation belts. Charged particles deflect or follow field lines to create auroras.

Indigenous Perspectives:
Many Northern Indigenous communities understand Auroras as the dancing spirits of ancestors. This profound connection physically manifests as charged particles dancing along the magnetic field lines.

Inquiry Questions

  • Why do solar particles loop around the Earth instead of hitting it directly?
  • How does increasing solar wind intensity change the shape of Earth's magnetic shield?
  • Why do auroras only occur near the planetary poles?
  • How does the visual magnetic axis differ from the rotational axis of Earth?
  • What is the relationship between the cosmic dancing auroras and Indigenous ancestor stories?

Conductor Magnetic Field

A straight wire carrying an electrical current generates a circular magnetic field around it, determined by the Right-Hand Rule. Field intensity is mapped to color (Blue=Weak, Green=Strong).

B = (μ0I) / (2πr)
Current (I) Magnitude 5 A
-10A0A+10A

Inquiry Questions

  • How does the field strength (B) change as you move the Gaussmeter radially away from the wire?
  • What is the relationship between current direction and the circular curl of the magnetic field?
  • Why is the field strength inversely proportional to the distance (r)?
  • How does switching the current from positive to negative alter the side-view dot/cross vectors?
  • If current is zero, what happens to the surrounding magnetic flux?

Fields & Solenoids

A solenoid produces a nearly uniform magnetic field inside its coils. The field is the superposition of the fields from each individual wire loop.

Inquiry Questions

  • Why is the magnetic field inside the center of the solenoid nearly uniform compared to the outside?
  • How does doubling the number of loops (N) affect the calculated magnetic field (B)?
  • What happens to the field direction inside the coils when current (I) is reversed?
  • How does the spacing of the coils influence the leakage of magnetic flux between loops?
  • Why do the fields from individual loops superimpose to create a stronger overall dipole?

Inquiry Questions

  • Why does a neutral particle (q=0) travel in a straight line through a magnetic field?
  • How does the sign of the charge (+q vs -q) determine the direction of the magnetic force deflection?
  • Why is the trajectory of a charged particle in a uniform magnetic field circular?
  • What happens to the radius of curvature of the path if you double the velocity (v)?
  • How does strengthening the external B-field affect the force vector magnitude and path tightness?
